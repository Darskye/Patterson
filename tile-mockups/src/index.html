A — Atelier
Concept A

Atelier

The high-end route. Near-black ground, brass detailing and a full-bleed bookmatched marble hero. This is the one carrying the heavy motion — it is built to feel expensive before a word is read.

  • Motion-led. Counter preloader, curtain wipe, word-by-word headline reveal, lerped smooth scroll, custom cursor and magnetic buttons.
  • Pinned horizontal gallery that scrolls sideways as you scroll down.
  • Drag-to-compare before/after on a real hall-bath rebuild.
  • Scroll-linked process line that fills as you move through the stages.

B — Journal
Concept B

Journal

The architectural-magazine route. Warm paper, clay accent, numbered sections and photographs presented as numbered plates. Calm and trustworthy rather than flashy — it reads like a craftsman's portfolio.

  • Editorial system. Numbered section tabs, drop cap, margin notes and captioned plates on an asymmetric grid.
  • Materials specimen book — Calacatta, travertine, hex mosaic and ledger stone as labelled swatches.
  • Services as a table of contents that expands in place.
  • Restrained motion: curtain reveals and slow parallax only.

C — Grid
Concept C

Grid

The contemporary route. Bone and ink on a hard modular grid with a kiln-orange accent — every block a tile. Confident, current, and the easiest of the three to extend with new sections later.

  • Bento capability grid mixing type blocks, photographs and colour fields.
  • A spec sheet — flatness, lippage, coverage, flood-test hold — that turns craftsmanship into something a client can actually check.
  • Grayscale work grid that snaps to full colour on hover.
  • Snappy mechanical motion: segmented progress, staggered tile reveals, number tickers.

The three marks

One business, three identities

Each mark is drawn as vector artwork, so it stays sharp at any size — from a favicon to the side of a van. All three work in a single colour, and all three come from the actual trade rather than generic “home services” iconography.

Before you send these on

What's real and what's placeholder

Accurate throughout

  • DTITile, the Atlanta Metro service area, 404.384.8819, and the site's own “precision and polish, detail and such” line.
  • Service categories drawn from the existing site: tile installation, bathroom remodels, kitchens, flooring, natural stone.

Placeholder — confirm with the client

  • Numbers (24 years, 1,400+ rooms, 18 communities) are illustrative.
  • Testimonials and project locations are written examples, not real reviews. Swap in genuine ones before launch.
  • Spec-sheet tolerances in concept C are industry-typical — check they match how DTI actually works.
  • Photography is AI-generated, styled on DTI's three existing job photos. Replace with real job photography before going live; the layouts are built to take it.
  • Forms are non-functional — they show the success state, nothing sends.
  • No photographs of the owner or crew are used anywhere.

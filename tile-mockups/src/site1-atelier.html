The standard

A tile floor is a plane. Either it is true, or it is not. There is no third option — and that is the whole of our trade.

Every job begins the same way: a flat substrate, a dry layout, a chalk line that answers to the room and not to the wall. We set for the eye — sightlines squared, cuts landed where they belong, grout joints that hold their width from the door to the far corner. It is slower. It is the only way we know.

How it goes

Four stages, no surprises

Stage 01

Walk & measure

We come to the house, measure the room, look at the substrate and tell you plainly what it needs. You get a written, itemised estimate — materials, labour, timeline — inside two days.

Stage 02

Layout & dry fit

Before a drop of mortar: substrate flattened, waterproofing applied and flood-tested, then the whole field snapped and dry-laid so you can see exactly where every cut will land.

Stage 03

Set

Back-buttered, levelled and clipped. One crew, ours, start to finish — the same hands on day one and day eleven. The site is swept and the water shut off every evening.

Stage 04

Grout, seal, walk-through

Joints packed full and struck clean, stone sealed, silicone at every change of plane. Then we walk it with you under a work light and fix anything you can find.
